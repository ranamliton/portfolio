Sex and Gender Variations in Patients of COVID-19 Epidemiology in Bangladesh

Home / publications / Project Details

Project Overview

This epidemiological study investigates the sex and gender-based differences in COVID-19 infection rates, severity, and outcomes among patients in Bangladesh. Understanding these disparities is crucial for developing targeted public health interventions and improving healthcare resource allocation.

Background

During the COVID-19 pandemic, emerging evidence from various countries suggested differences in infection rates and disease severity between males and females. However, data specific to the Bangladeshi population was limited. This study aimed to fill that gap by analyzing national and regional health data.

Objectives

  • To analyze sex-disaggregated COVID-19 data from Bangladesh
  • To identify patterns in infection rates between males and females
  • To compare disease severity and mortality rates by sex
  • To explore socioeconomic and behavioral factors contributing to observed differences

Methodology

The study utilized a comprehensive data analysis approach:

  1. Data Collection: COVID-19 case data was gathered from official health portals and international databases including WHO, Johns Hopkins University, and IEDCR Bangladesh.
  2. Comparative Analysis: Data from Bangladesh was compared with neighboring South Asian countries and global trends.
  3. Statistical Analysis: SPSS was used for statistical testing including chi-square tests and regression analysis.
  4. Visualization: Graphs and charts were generated to illustrate temporal trends and demographic patterns.

Key Findings

The analysis revealed notable differences in COVID-19 epidemiology between sexes in Bangladesh. Males showed higher infection rates in certain age groups, while females faced unique challenges related to healthcare access. The study also highlighted the importance of gender-sensitive public health messaging and resource distribution.

Technology Used

SPSS Data Analysis Epidemiological Modeling WHO Data Johns Hopkins Database Statistical Testing

Impact

This research contributed to the growing body of evidence on sex-disaggregated health data in low-resource settings. The findings were shared with public health stakeholders to inform gender-responsive COVID-19 policies in Bangladesh.
