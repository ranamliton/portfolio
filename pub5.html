Shotgun Metagenomic Profiling Reveals Bacillus-Dominated Bacterial Communities in Urban Rooftop and Surface Garden Soils of Bangladesh

Home / Publications / Project Details

Project Overview

This study employed shotgun whole-metagenome sequencing (WMS) to characterize bacterial diversity and community structure in 27 soil samples from rooftop and surface gardens across Dhaka and Gazipur, Bangladesh. The research reveals the ecological dominance of Bacillus species in urban agricultural soils and uncovers location- and garden-type-specific patterns that influence soil functionality, with significant implications for sustainable urban agriculture and food security.

Introduction

Urban rooftop and surface garden systems play a critical role in food security in densely populated regions, yet their soil microbiomes remain understudied. To date, no baseline data exists on rooftop and surface garden soil microbiomes in Bangladesh. Understanding these communities is vital for enhancing soil health, nutrient cycling, and resilience for sustainable, climate-adapted urban agriculture.

Urbanization profoundly alters land use, impacting surface landscapes and the physicochemical and microbial properties of soil. These changes threaten soil biodiversity and the biological processes essential for ecosystem functionality. Soil microorganisms drive nutrient cycling, decompose organic matter, maintain plant diversity, enhance soil structure, and suppress diseases. Within this microbiome, the genus Bacillus is one of the most ubiquitous and ecologically significant bacterial genera, exhibiting strong genetic and metabolic plasticity.

In Bangladesh, rooftop gardening is becoming increasingly popular, yet the microbial ecology of these systems remains poorly understood. The potential for microbial contamination of fruits and vegetables grown in these gardens poses a significant health risk, particularly in densely populated urban areas where food safety is a growing concern.

Study Design and Methodology

The study analyzed 27 soil samples collected from rooftop and surface gardens in Dhaka and Gazipur districts, Bangladesh:

  • Dhaka Rooftop Gardens (DRG): 7 samples
  • Dhaka Surface Gardens (DSG): 6 samples
  • Gazipur Rooftop Gardens (GRG): 8 samples
  • Gazipur Surface Gardens (GSG): 6 samples

These gardens, located 15–20 km apart (23.72–23.99°N, 90.40-90.42°E), experience a tropical wet and dry climate with 1,875 mm average rainfall, 26.1°C mean annual temperature, and 65.8% humidity. Composite soil samples (0–5 cm depth) were aseptically collected during April–July 2024.

Shotgun whole-metagenome sequencing (WMS) was performed using Illumina NovaSeq 6000, generating 1.34 billion reads (avg. 49.8 M/sample). DNA was extracted using Qiagen DNeasy PowerSoil Kit, and libraries were prepared with Nextera XT DNA Library Preparation Kit. Bioinformatics analysis was conducted using CZ ID for taxonomic classification, MEGAHIT for assembly, and KEGG for functional pathway annotation.

Key Findings

Bacterial Diversity and Composition

WMS analysis revealed distinct microbiome profiles across garden types:

  • Firmicutes dominated (65.30–83.16% relative abundance), followed by Proteobacteria (3.42–24.92%)
  • Bacillus species emerged as the most dominant and ubiquitous genus, consistently comprising over 53% of the total bacteriome across all soils
  • A total of 755 bacterial species were identified across all samples
  • 19 phyla, 31 classes, 75 orders, 122 families, 275 genera, and 755 species were detected

Alpha and Beta Diversity Patterns

Diversity analysis revealed important ecological patterns:

  • Alpha diversity (within-sample) showed no significant differences between garden types or locations (p > 0.05), indicating similar bacterial richness and evenness
  • Beta diversity (between-sample) revealed significant community structuring (p = 0.017, Kruskal-Wallis test), with distinct clustering by location and garden type
  • Surface gardens harbored greater bacterial species richness (DSG: 717, GSG: 750 species) compared to rooftop gardens (DRG: 699, GRG: 730 species)
  • Bacteroidota was significantly higher in surface gardens (DSG: 11.54%, GSG: 2.69%) compared to rooftop gardens (DRG: 1.2%, GRG: 0.73%)

Location-Specific Bacillus Species Distribution

Of 41 Bacillus species detected, 58.54% were shared across all garden types, yet their abundances varied significantly with microhabitat:

  • DRG (Dhaka Rooftop): Dominated by B. paralicheniformis (28.3%), B. licheniformis (25.2%), and B. cereus sensu lato (8.6%)
  • DSG (Dhaka Surface): Characterized by B. cereus sensu lato (16.0%), Brevibacillus agri (12.1%), and Flavobacterium thermophilum (11.4%)
  • GRG (Gazipur Rooftop): Dominated by B. cereus sensu lato (42.4%), B. agri (11.5%), and B. altitudinis (5.4%)
  • GSG (Gazipur Surface): Enriched with B. stratosphericus (14.6%), B. licheniformis (12.7%), B. safensis (9.7%), and B. altitudinis (8.8%)

KEGG Functional Pathway Analysis

KEGG functional profiling revealed marked metabolic stratification among garden soil bacteriomes:

  • Carbohydrate metabolism dominated all sites (9.30–11.07%)
  • DRG was uniquely enriched in photosynthesis (8.40%) and methane metabolism (8.62%), indicative of a phototroph-driven consortium adapted to the isolated, high-light rooftop niche
  • DSG, GRG, and GSG showed higher oxidative phosphorylation (3.75–4.08%), two-component systems (3.24–3.73%), and biosynthetic pathways, reflecting heterotroph-dominated profiles adapted to competitive, resource-rich surface soil environments
  • ABC transporters (3.56–4.33%) and biosynthesis of amino acids (2.41–4.30%) were among the top pathways across all groups

Implications and Significance

This research has critical implications for sustainable urban agriculture:

  • Soil Health Management: The dominance of Bacillus species highlights their potential as biofertilizer candidates to enhance crop resilience and soil productivity in urban settings
  • Location-Specific Strategies: The distinct Bacillus profiles between rooftop and surface gardens underscore the need for tailored soil management practices based on garden type and geographic location
  • Food Security: Understanding soil microbiomes is essential for optimizing urban agriculture systems that contribute to food security in densely populated regions like Bangladesh
  • Biotechnological Applications: The prevalence of resilient, spore-forming Bacillus species with plant growth-promoting traits offers opportunities for developing microbiome-assisted biofertilizers and biocontrol agents
  • Public Health: The presence of B. cereus sensu lato in some samples warrants attention regarding potential food safety risks in urban agricultural systems

Conclusion

This study provides the first comprehensive shotgun metagenomic characterization of bacteriomes in Bangladeshi urban garden soils, revealing the ecological dominance of Bacillus species with location-driven compositional and functional shifts. While alpha diversity was consistent across sites, beta diversity revealed distinct community structuring driven by a combination of geographic location and garden type.

The findings are pivotal for optimizing sustainable urban agriculture in rapidly developing regions, where soil bacteriomes can be harnessed to improve crop resilience and food security. The study highlights the importance of location-specific soil management strategies and the potential of Bacillus-based biofertilizers for enhancing urban agricultural productivity. Future research should focus on isolating dominant strains, validating their plant growth-promoting traits, and evaluating their field performance to bridge the gap between microbial ecology and sustainable urban farming.
