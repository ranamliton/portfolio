Genome-wide Analysis of Extended-Spectrum Beta-Lactamase-Producing Escherichia coli from Seafood in Bangladesh: Population Structure, Resistome, Virulome, and Global Dissemination Patterns

Home / Publications / Publication Details

Overview

This study provides the first comprehensive genomic characterization of ESBL-producing Escherichia coli from seafood in Bangladesh. Using whole-genome sequencing of 10 isolates from crab, shrimp, and tuna, combined with comparative analysis against 722 global genomes, the research reveals significant genetic diversity, multidrug resistance, and the presence of key virulence factors. The findings highlight the potential of seafood to act as a reservoir and transmission vehicle for antimicrobial resistance.

Introduction

Seafood is widely consumed worldwide due to its unique sensory attributes and high nutritional value. Global seafood consumption has more than doubled in the past five decades. However, seafood is particularly vulnerable to microbial contamination because of its direct contact with aquatic ecosystems that may receive untreated waste and industrial effluents.

Among seafood-borne pathogens, Escherichia coli is of special importance. While many strains are commensal, pathogenic variants can cause gastroenteritis, urinary tract infections, and septicemia. Consuming raw or undercooked seafood contaminated with E. coli can lead to outbreaks of foodborne illness.

Beyond pathogenicity, seafood-associated E. coli may serve as reservoirs of antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs) that can be horizontally transferred within bacterial populations. Of particular concern is resistance to β-lactam antibiotics mediated by extended-spectrum β-lactamases (ESBLs). Bangladesh is one of the world's largest exporters of seafood, making it essential to monitor bacterial contamination and AMR.

Study Design and Methodology

The study employed a comprehensive genomic approach:

  • Sample Collection: 10 E. coli isolates from seafood (crab = 3, shrimp = 1, tuna = 6)
  • Sequencing: Whole-genome sequencing using Oxford Nanopore Technologies PromethION platform
  • Library Prep: Rapid Barcoding Kit 96 V14Q20+ Kit14 with R10.4.1 flow cell
  • Assembly: Flye assembler followed by Medaka polishing
  • Global Comparison: Systematic PubMed search identified 722 global genomes of beta-lactamase-producing E. coli
  • Bioinformatics: MLST, cgMLST, serotyping, ANI, pangenome analysis, ARG and VG profiling

Key Findings

Genomic Features and Typing

Bioinformatics analysis revealed diverse genomic characteristics:

  • Genome sizes ranged from 4,048,090 to 4,961,915 bp
  • GC contents varied between 50.5% and 50.89%
  • Most isolates displayed unique sequence types (STs) and core genome sequence types (cgSTs)
  • Three isolates shared both ST1431 and cgST104784, indicating close genetic relatedness
  • Shrimp and tuna isolates possessed both O and H antigens, while crab isolates carried only single antigen types

Pangenome Analysis

Pangenome analysis demonstrated remarkable genomic plasticity:

  • Strain-specific genes: 56.4%
  • Dispensable genes: 34.2%
  • Core genes: 9.4%
  • Functional categories dominated by metabolism, followed by cellular processes and signaling
  • Highest strain-specific genes found in crab isolates (MTR_EC05: 4,246 genes)

Antimicrobial Resistance Genes (ARGs)

Several ARGs were identified across all isolates:

  • AmpC beta-lactamase present in all isolates
  • CTX-M-15 found in MTR_EC03 (crab)
  • blaDHA-1 in MTR_EC02 (crab)
  • Fluoroquinolone resistance (qnrS1, qnrB4) in MTR_EC02
  • Tetracycline resistance (tetA) in all genomes except MTR_EC02 and MTR_EC05
  • Sulfonamide resistance (sul1) in MTR_EC02
  • Vancomycin resistance (vanG) in all except MTR_EC02 and MTR_EC05
  • Extensive efflux pump systems (MFS, ABC transporters) across all genomes

Virulence Genes (VGs)

A substantial number of virulence genes were found with variation across isolates:

  • Adherence genes: Curli fibers (csg operon) detected in tuna isolates
  • Fimbrial operon: Nearly complete fimA-fimI identified across all genomes
  • Autotransporter genes: Variable distribution including tibA, aida, agn43
  • Iron uptake genes: sitA, fes, entA-entF present in almost all genomes
  • Toxin genes: hlyE/clyA in all except MTR_EC05; astA in MTR_ES05 and MTR_ET09
  • Tuna-derived isolates harbored the highest abundance of adherence genes

Mobile Genetic Elements and Plasmids

Plasmids were detected exclusively in crab isolates:

  • MTR_EC02: Multiple plasmid types (IncFII, IncFIA, IncFIB(pHCM2), ColRNAI) carrying ARGs [qnrB4, dfrA17, qacE, mph(A), sul1, blaDHA-1]
  • MTR_EC03 and MTR_EC05: Col440I plasmid only
  • Plasmids were absent in shrimp and tuna isolates
  • Genomic islands and prophages harbored ARGs and VGs across all isolates

Population Structure and Global Phylogeny

Comparative analysis with 722 global genomes revealed:

  • 108,456 SNPs observed following core genome alignment
  • Bangladeshi isolates formed clusters with global strains from Ecuador, Czech Republic, USA, Singapore, Germany, and Pakistan
  • ST345 closely matched wastewater (Czech Republic) and human (USA) isolates
  • ST10 is recognized as a "high-risk" sequence type in extraintestinal clinical infections
  • Limited serotype concordance with international strains, suggesting localized evolution

Implications and Significance

This research has critical implications for food safety and public health:

  • First Genomic Study: This is the first comprehensive genomic characterization of ESBL-producing E. coli from seafood in Bangladesh
  • Seafood as AMR Reservoir: Seafood products represent high-risk vehicles for transmitting antimicrobial-resistant E. coli
  • Global Dissemination: Phylogenetic links with international strains suggest potential contamination through trade networks or shared environmental reservoirs
  • One Health Interventions: Urgent need for coordinated interventions addressing the aquaculture-environment-human interface
  • Policy Recommendations: Strengthened AMR surveillance, strict hygiene practices, elimination of prophylactic antibiotic use in aquaculture, and harmonized international food safety standards

Conclusion

This study provides the first comprehensive genomic characterization of ESBL-producing E. coli from seafood in Bangladesh, revealing significant genetic diversity, multidrug resistance, and the presence of key virulence factors. The detection of diverse MLSTs, β-lactamase genes such as blaCTX-M-15, ampC, and blaDHA-1, and plasmid-mediated ARGs underscores the potential of seafood to act as a reservoir and transmission vehicle for AMR. Comparative genomic analysis demonstrated strong links between Bangladeshi isolates and global strains from humans, animals, and the environment, highlighting the interconnectedness of AMR dissemination through food systems and aquatic environments. These findings emphasize the urgent need for strengthened AMR surveillance and strict hygiene practices in aquaculture and seafood processing, guided by a One Health approach.
